Steward ships fourteen themes as seven light/dark families. They are not
variations on a house style — gruvbox, Catppuccin, Rosé Pine, Tokyo Night,
GitHub, Flexoki and Kanagawa are other people's palettes, transcribed rather
than invented, and the app has no colours of its own outside them.

What is Steward's is the **set of roles** every palette has to fill, and the
**floors** every role is held to. A theme is a table of hexes; the roles are the
contract.

## Roles, not colours

Surfaces come in four tiers — `bg` is the page, `bg1` the card, `bg2` and `bg3`
the raised and sunken variants. Ink comes in three — `ink` for body, `inkDim`
for metadata, `inkFaint` for glyphs. Borders come in three — `borderDim` for
hairlines between planes, `border` for object edges, `borderStrong` for the
boundary of a control that has no fill of its own.

The tiers are ordered, and the ordering is asserted rather than assumed. A
`border` louder than the `borderStrong` it sits inside inverts the hierarchy,
which is how every light theme's border drifted to within one ramp step of its
own canvas before anything held them apart.

## The mark is not in here

The identity does not follow the active theme, in either framing. The bare
glyph has two colourways keyed on light or dark mode; the chip has exactly one.
See [the mark](/docs/design/mark) — the surfaces below are what the glyph is
measured _against_, not something it participates in.

### The tiers

Every theme carries the same roles, and every role is held to the same floor on
every theme. The floors are asserted in `theme.test.ts`, per theme, so a new
palette cannot ship a tier that inverts or a surface that vanishes.

| held                            | floor, and why                                |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| `ink` on every surface          | 4.5:1 — body copy, full AA                    |
| `inkDim` on every surface       | 4.5:1 — no dimmer text tier exists (ADR-0048) |
| `inkFaint` on every surface     | 3:1 — a glyph role, WCAG 1.4.11               |
| `borderStrong` on page and card | 3:1 — fill-less controls are only their edge  |
| `border` on page and card       | 1.5:1 — object edges                          |
| `borderDim` on page and card    | 1.2:1 — plane hairlines                       |
| `bg` against `bg1`              | 1.05:1 — a card must sit off the page         |

Two of those are ordering rather than absolute: `inkDim` may never out-contrast
`ink`, and the three border tiers must stay strictly ranked. A divider louder
than the card edge it sits inside is a hierarchy inversion, and it is the defect
every light theme drifted into before the tiers were held.

## Adding a palette

Transcribe it; don't invent rows. Then run the suite — `theme.test.ts` holds
every floor above against the new theme automatically, because it iterates the
registry rather than listing themes. A palette that cannot clear the floors
does not ship, and the usual failure is a light theme whose borders are too
close to its own paper.
