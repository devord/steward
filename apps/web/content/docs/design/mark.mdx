The mark is a bow tie: two wings meeting at a pinched waist, the butler's
uniform in one shape. It has two framings, and they are different objects
rather than one drawing with and without a tile.

**In chrome** — the rail, the header, the account bar — it is the **bare
glyph**: flat ember, level, on whatever surface it is handed. This is the one
framing sitting on a ground it does not own, so it is the one thing keyed on
light or dark mode.

**On display surfaces** — the browser tab, the launcher, the social card, the
README lockup — it wears the **chip**: the identity drenched across a
superellipse tile with the bow cut out of it in paper, turned 12°, because a
bow tie is worn rather than laid flat. The chip is a single colourway in both
modes. It is a saturated object, not a surface; it isn't borrowing the page's
tone, so it has no reason to follow the page's mode.

## There is no knot

The mark was three shapes for a year, and the third one was the whole problem.
The knot's only job was to be a different colour from the cloth it lay on, and
at favicon size that boundary was four pixels wide and measured **1.40:1** —
while every contrast test in the suite passed, because they all held ink
against _ground_ and none held ink against ink.

It could not be fixed with colour. The knot protruded past the waist, so it
touched the page as well as the cloth, and nothing clears both:

| dark colourway  | vs the wing | vs the page |
| --------------- | ----------- | ----------- |
| pure black knot | 5.43 ✓      | 1.02 ✗      |
| pure white knot | 2.53 ✗      | 14.74 ✓     |

Dropping it doesn't solve that problem, it deletes it: one shape has no
interior edge to hold, no sub-pixel feature to keep, nothing to measure. The
waist carries the read. [ADR-0053](/docs/architecture) has the full argument.

### What is measured, and where it is worst

The bare glyph is one shape, so it has exactly one boundary: itself against the
surface it was handed. Each colourway is measured only against the surfaces it
can actually land on — page and card, of every theme in its mode:

|                         | worst      | on                     |
| ----------------------- | ---------- | ---------------------- |
| glyph on light surfaces | **4.30:1** | tokyo-night-light page |
| glyph on dark surfaces  | **5.84:1** | gruvbox-dark page      |

The chip takes no mode, so its tile faces all 14 themes. It carries no border,
and **neither gradient stop holds an edge alone** — they fail on opposite
grounds, which is exactly why the gradient runs diagonally and puts both stops
on the perimeter. What is held is that at least one clears:

|                       | worst  | on                     |
| --------------------- | ------ | ---------------------- |
| tile, top of the fold | 2.72:1 | tokyo-night-light page |
| tile, deep end        | 2.41:1 | gruvbox-dark page      |

The chip's own interior edge, and its habitat — the surfaces it lands on that
are not Steward's:

| ------------------------------- | ------ |
| bow against the tile's top      | 3.41:1 |
| bow against the tile's deep end | 5.40:1 |
| tile on Chrome, light           | 4.67:1 |
| tile on Chrome, dark            | 4.16:1 |
| tile on GitHub, light           | 6.12:1 |

## Why the gradient is not decoration

The chip carries no border. It had one only because the old tile sat within a
hair of the page tone, and at 16px a one-unit stroke on a 64-unit tile is a
quarter of a device pixel — a feature that never rendered on the surface it
existed for.

Drenched, the tile holds its own edge, but neither gradient stop does that
alone: they fail on opposite grounds. The top clears every dark surface and
drops on a pale one; the deep end clears every light surface and drops on a
dark one. Running the gradient diagonally puts both stops on the perimeter, so
at least one part of the edge always clears the floor. That is the load the
diagonal carries, and it's asserted rather than assumed.

## Two gates, and why neither is enough

`theme.test.ts` holds **every visible boundary** at 3:1 — not every ink against
its background, which is a different set and is the set that let a 1.40:1 edge
ship. `mark.test.ts` holds **every drawn feature at one device pixel** at the
declared minimum, because contrast says two regions differ and never says
either is big enough to be a region.

Both can fail automatically. Neither can tell you whether the thing reads as a
bow tie, so `brand/proof/` holds renders at true device pixels, magnified with
nearest-neighbour — what you enlarge is the real raster, not a clean re-render
of the vector at a larger size. Regenerate them with `node scripts/mark-sheet.ts`.

The sheets cover Steward's fourteen themes and, separately, the surfaces the
chip actually lands on: Chrome's and GitHub's light and dark chrome. A favicon
tested only against its own app's themes has not been tested.
